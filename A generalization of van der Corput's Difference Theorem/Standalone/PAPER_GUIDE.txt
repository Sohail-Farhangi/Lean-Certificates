A GENERALIZATION OF VAN DER CORPUT'S DIFFERENCE THEOREM

Reader's guide to VdCStandalone.lean
Sohail Farhangi, arXiv:2106.01123v6 (15 August 2024)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01123v6

Start with cube_spectral_difference_theorem, cube_weakMixing_difference_theorem,
and cube_orthogonal_iff_lag_pairs. Search for each exact Lean declaration name
below. Each source marker in the standalone file identifies the corresponding
module in the modular project. References such as Cube.lean below refer to
these source markers.

To check the file, open a terminal in the sibling Lean-Pool-and-Palomar folder
and run these commands with elan and Lake.

    lake exe cache get
    lake env lean -DwarningAsError=true -R ../Standalone ../Standalone/VdCStandalone.lean

The file imports Mathlib and uses Lean 4.36.0 with Mathlib commit
fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842. It contains the proof library,
including the checked examples and cited thesis theorem. The separate
Palomar Challenge and Solution are in the modular package.

NOTATION AND QUANTIFIERS

VdC/Basic.lean — Lean sequences have domain Nat. The paper's term x_n becomes
x (n-1). Thus `Finset.range N` is precisely the first N terms. `cesaro` and
`realCesaro` divide by N. The value at N=0 has no effect on the limits. In
`corr x y h N` the shift is on x and `star` conjugates y. A positive lag in a
lag average or a series is written `h + 1` with h ranging over Nat.

`PermissibleTriple x y N` requires bounded sequences and a strictly increasing
N, with limits for the four ordered pairs xx, xy, yx and yy at every
nonnegative lag. `CorrLimits` records the limit function itself. A refinement
keeps the original increasing N and composes it with one increasing selector.
In particular, the refinement depends on the family of correlations, and the
same selector works at all lags. For each h, `limsup ... atTop` is over the
averaging length N before summing or averaging over h.

`CompactAlong` uses finite covers in the limiting squared shift distance. Each
positive shift m may use one of finitely many positive shifts k, with limiting
distance less than epsilon. A bound by epsilon/2 in this formulation gives the
strict inequality for the supremum in the manuscript. `CompactSeq` uses the
identity sequence of averaging lengths. `NearlyWeakMixing` averages the norm
of the already existing cross-correlation limits.

`measureFourier μ h` takes the coefficient with the negative Fourier sign.
`HasSpectrum r μ` matches the nonnegative coefficients against r. μ is a
positive finite measure on the unit circle. Absolute continuity is written
`μ ≪ circleHaar`. The theorem `exists_integrable_density` verifies its density
formulation.
`MutuallySingular circleHaar` expresses the singular spectrum condition. The
circle identifies the endpoints of a fundamental interval. Lean uses the
complex autocorrelation itself in the spectral definitions, as required by
the spectral-measure identity. The printed formulas (12) and (15) show
absolute-value bars on the autocorrelation. The guide distinguishes this
convention from the absolute values used in the discrepancy hypotheses.

`Equidistributed μ x` tests convergence against every complex continuous
function, with integral against μ. `testMean μ f = 0` is the paper's mean-zero
condition. `torusHaar d` is the d-fold Haar probability, and `cubeLebesgue d`
is Lebesgue probability on the actual closed cube. For products of cubes the
certificate uses the product measure on two coordinate blocks.

SECTION 2 — PRELIMINARIES

Definition 2.1 (DefinitionOfDiscrepancy)
  Rectangles.lean defines finite discrepancies over closed axis-parallel
  rectangles by a supremum of absolute frequency-volume errors. The volume
  is the product of side lengths. Difference sequences use their coordinatewise
  [0,1) representatives. `upperDifferenceDiscrepancy` takes the length limsup
  at each fixed lag. The certificate includes the finite bound 0 ≤ D_N ≤ 1.
  RectangleMeasure.lean identifies the volume with cube Lebesgue measure,
  identifies the empirical frequency with an indicator average, and proves
  domination by any discrepancy bound over a family containing the closed
  rectangles. `equidistributed_iff_rectangleDiscrepancy_tendsto_zero` in
  ThesisWeakMixing.lean links the definition to continuous-test uniform
  distribution.

Definition 2.4 (PermissibleTriplesDefinition)
  Basic.lean defines the triple. `exists_common_subsequence` and
  `exists_permissible_refinement` in Permissible.lean construct refinements
  using a countable product of closed complex discs. The discrepancy-only
  permissible-pair definition of the paper is used in the separate thesis
  theorem discussed below.

Definitions 2.5 and 2.6 (MixingAndRigidSequencesDefinition / MixingAndRigidDistributionsDefinitions)
  Basic.lean and SpectralSequences.lean define the five scalar classes.
  Distribution.lean and Applications.lean define the corresponding continuous
  test conditions. CompactSpectrum.lean proves `spectrallySingular_of_compactSeq`.
  MixingHierarchy.lean verifies the spectral Lebesgue to weak mixing inclusion,
  and Applications.lean gives weak mixing to equidistribution.

Spectral realization related to Lemma 2.9 (WhyWeNeedPermissibleTriples)
  Realization.lean uses finite Fourier polynomials and positive measures instead
  of the manuscript's Hilbert space embedding. `exists_corr_spectrum` realizes
  one autocorrelation function. `exists_joint_corr_spectrum` uses the same
  permissible subsequence to realize the spectrum of every linear combination
  of a finite family. The negative coefficients come from the reversed ordered
  pair and conjugation. The polynomial density is squared modulus divided by N.
  The resulting Fourier coefficients differ from the lag sums only by an end
  correction that tends to zero. Weak compactness yields the limiting measure.

Topological part of Lemma 2.10 (RepresentationLemma)
  `bounded_sequence_orbit_representation` in OrbitRepresentation.lean realizes a
  bounded sequence as a continuous coordinate on its compact metrizable shift
  orbit closure. This covers the topological representation assertion.

Lemma 2.11 (CreatingSpectrallyLebesgue/NWMSequences)
  `spectrallyLebesgue_of_limsup_square_summable` in SpectralResults.lean bounds the
  squares of subsequence coefficients by the full limsups. SpectralAC.lean uses
  the circle's L² Fourier basis and uniqueness of Fourier coefficients of
  measures to obtain absolute continuity. `nearlyWeakMixing_of_upper_corr` in
  Permissible.lean converts the Cesàro-small autocorrelation majorant into the
  required Cesàro-small cross-correlations with every bounded test sequence.

Lemma 2.12 (ClosedUnderLinearClosureLemma)
  Scalar addition and multiplication appear in SpectralResults.lean,
  SpectralScalars.lean, Permissible.lean and CompactClosure.lean. The
  mean-square closure theorems allow a different approximant at each tolerance.
  The eventual mean-square error bound follows from an upper mean-square
  error smaller than that tolerance. The singular closure argument uses a
  countable union of null supports for the approximants.

Corollary 2.13 and Weyl's criterion (CheckingForwm/sL/c/ss)
  Weyl's criterion is `equidistributed_torus_iff` in Torus.lean. The two
  `...Distribution_iff_torus_characters` theorems in FourierTests.lean extend the
  wm and sL tests from nonzero characters to all continuous mean-zero torus
  tests. Stone–Weierstrass yields uniform approximation by trigonometric
  polynomials after centering. The cube versions are `cube_sLebesgue_iff_characters`
  and `cube_weakMixing_iff_characters` in CubeDifference.lean. Cube.lean uses
  continuous cutoffs that vanish at the boundary and tend to one in the
  interior. Their periodic extensions allow centered cube tests to be
  approximated in empirical mean square, including for sequences that contain
  cube endpoints. HalfFrequency.lean uses the embedding x ↦ x/2 mod 1 to
  obtain the half-frequency density. `cube_compact_iff_centeredHalfFrequencies`
  and `cube_sSingular_iff_centeredHalfFrequencies` use exactly the nonzero
  centered half-frequency tests g_m from the manuscript.

Theorem 2.3 (SpecialCaseOfKHInequality)
  `finite_fourier_rectangle_bound` in RectangleTransfer.lean is the cited
  finite estimate, with `fourierDiscrepancyConstant m = (4π M)^d` and M the
  largest magnitude among the frequency coordinates. RectangleFourier.lean
  expands the character into integrals over coordinate thresholds. The finite
  sample errors are bounded by the discrepancy of the associated rectangles.

Lemma 2.14 and modulo-one sum application (ErgodicDichotomyForSequences)
  SpectralOrthogonality.lean proves the measure-level orthogonality of an
  absolutely continuous and a singular component. SpectralResults.lean and
  SpectralScalars.lean recover the scalar product limit for these two classes.
  `nearlyWeakMixing_mul_compact` in Permissible.lean handles compactness along
  M directly, including M=id. Applications.lean applies both results to
  characters for the modulo-one sum corollary.

SECTION 3 — MAIN RESULTS

Theorem 3.1(i) (PropertiesOfsLSequences)
  `equidistributed_selected_of_sl_singular` and
  `equidistributed_selected_of_wm_compact` in Applications.lean. The manuscript
  includes positive lower density in the definition of a singular or compact
  sequence of natural numbers. This is an explicit hypothesis in Lean.
  `hasPositiveLowerDensity_iff` verifies the equivalence with positivity of
  the liminf of the empirical range densities.
  Selection.lean uses the identity that at averaging length σ(K)+1 the range
  of σ has exactly K+1 points. The positive lower-density bound controls the
  factor changing the normalization from σ(K)+1 to K+1.

Theorem 3.1(iii) (PropertiesOfsLSequences)
  `equidistributed_pair_of_sl_singular` and `equidistributed_pair_of_wm_compact`
  in Applications.lean. The second coordinate is assumed equidistributed.
  The proofs apply the dichotomy to tests with zero mean on the first
  coordinate, then add back the two means for a general product test.
  `equidistributed_prod_iff` extends product tests to all continuous tests
  by Stone–Weierstrass. The two parts hold on the cube with `cubeLebesgue`,
  and are expressed for compact probability spaces more generally.

Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 (GeneralizationOfClassicalvdC / WeakMixingUDvdC)
  The main statements are `cube_spectral_difference_theorem` and
  `cube_weakMixing_difference_theorem` in CubeDifference.lean. Their only
  substantive hypothesis on the sequence is the manuscript's square
  summability or Cesàro hypothesis, respectively. Difference.lean checks the
  passage from finite bounds to lagwise limsups. Rectangles.lean supplies the
  finite discrepancies. The intermediate torus statements in FourierTests.lean
  accept a `CharacterRectangleEstimate`, which `characterRectangleEstimate_all`
  in RectangleTransfer.lean proves for every sequence. The cube conclusions
  concern all continuous mean-zero tests on the closed cube.

Theorem 3.4 (4CharacterizationsOfwmSequences)
  `thesis_four_characterizations_weakMixing` in ThesisWeakMixing.lean is
  Theorem 2.4.17 of Sohail Farhangi's Topics in Ergodic Theory and Ramsey
  Theory (The Ohio State University, 2022). Reading it from the paper's
  item (i), the three equivalences are with `ThesisCrossDiscrepancy` (ii),
  `ThesisSelfDiscrepancy` (iii), and `ThesisDifferenceDiscrepancy` (iv).
  `PermissiblePair` uses a single strictly increasing sequence of averaging
  lengths on which one discrepancy limit exists at each positive lag.
  Lean uses closed rectangles and positive dimensions in the theorem. The
  cross criterion quantifies over every uniformly distributed comparison
  sequence in every positive dimension, and shifts that comparison sequence.
  The proof uses a finite Fourier test estimate proved by compactness and
  Portmanteau, and uses one common refinement for all lags. The reverse
  correlation estimate in Permissible.lean handles the shift on the second
  sequence when applying the definition of nearly weakly mixing.

  Primary source
  https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/r/etd/search/10?p10_accession_num=osu165203490336716

Theorem 3.5 (CharacterizationOfOsequences)
  `orthogonalDistribution_iff_lag_pairs` in LagPairs.lean and
  `cube_orthogonal_iff_lag_pairs` in Cube.lean. From nearly orthogonal centered
  tests, Orthogonal.lean uses a finite shift window to prove the mean vanishes.
  Polarization with f+g and f+i*g obtains the mixed positive-lag correlations.
  In the reverse direction one tests each joint lagged pair against
  conjugate(f) ⊗ f. The cube specialization retains all positive lags.

SECTION 4 — EXAMPLES

Lemma 4.1 (LemmaConstructingSequences)
  `ae_constructingSequences_cube` in ErgodicCubeExamples.lean gives all six
  assertions on one conull set for the given system and the given positive
  measure set A. `ae_tendsto_orbitCesaro` in BoundedPET.lean proves the bounded
  pointwise ergodic theorem used in the argument. ErgodicExamples.lean passes
  to the common full-measure set for all continuous tests and all lags, and
  proves convergence of the frequencies of return times. ErgodicWeakMixing.lean
  obtains the scalar Cesàro criterion from ergodicity of T × T.
  ErgodicDiscrete.lean starts with density of the span of the actual L²
  Koopman eigenvectors and uses bounded eigenfunction representatives for
  the compactness conclusion. The Lebesgue and singular cases express the
  system's hypotheses by absolutely continuous and singular spectral measures
  for the corresponding observables. Orbit and visit indices start at zero.

Thue–Morse and the generalized digit-sum residue lemma
  `thueMorse_positive_spectrallySingular` gives the binary example.
  `spectrallySingular_digitResidue_enumeration` gives the general base r ≥ 2
  and residue 0 ≤ j < r. `tendsto_digitResidue_density` proves natural density
  exactly 1/r. DigitExamples.lean gives one point per block of length r and
  proves the roots-of-unity filter. DigitCorrelation.lean checks the carry
  recursion on complete digit blocks and proves convergence of the full
  autocorrelation averages. DigitLacunary.lean and DigitSpectrum.lean use
  the coefficients at r^k and r^k−1 to distinguish the spectral measure from
  Haar measure by two different mean-square limits of the same continuous
  tests. SpectralSeparation.lean proves that this distinction gives mutual
  singularity. The certificate proves the spectral input for the characters
  used by the residue filter. Lean uses the
  base-r digit word indexed starting at zero, so the residue-zero selection
  includes 0. Omitting that initial entry gives the paper's positive-index
  enumeration and has no effect on the density or spectral limits.

Lemma 4.2 (ExampleNecessitatingPermissibleTriples)
  `exampleNecessitatingPermissibleTriples` in DuplicationExample.lean proves
  the existence of an sL-sequence with upper discrepancy exactly 1/2 at every
  positive lag. `duplicationCube_example` works for every irrational quadratic
  coefficient, and the existential statement uses √2. Lean keeps the paper's
  doubled-square construction, uses Cantor pairing to repeat the block lengths,
  and chooses stage lengths to dominate finitely many geometric-sum bounds.
  DuplicationEstimates.lean shows that the masked Fourier averages tend to
  zero at any fixed positive lag. For each prefix, each index contributes to
  at most one intentional coincidence lag. DuplicationSpectrum.lean uses this
  to get summable autocorrelation magnitudes in each permissible limit.
  DuplicationDiscrepancy.lean removes coincident positions, proves uniform
  distribution of the retained differences, and bounds the erased fraction
  by 1/2 + h/(2N) for lag h. The lower bound comes from the stages at which the
  prescribed length matches h and half the entries are repeated.
  `duplicationCube_not_orthogonal` gives the consequence that this sequence
  fails the o-sequence criterion.

Lemma 4.3 (PolynomialLikeCounterExample)
  `polynomialLikeCounterExample` in PolynomialExample.lean verifies that the
  piecewise-quadratic sequence is sL, that its differences are uniformly
  distributed at every positive lag, and that its lag pairs are uniformly
  distributed exactly when h > 1. It verifies the resulting failure of the
  o-sequence condition. `polynomialLike_lag_one_fourier_half` gives the explicit
  limiting Fourier coefficient 1/2 at frequency (2,−1). PolynomialPhases.lean
  proves the needed linear and quadratic equidistribution estimates, and
  ResidueAverages.lean gives the parity decomposition of the Cesàro averages.

INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE

The classical difference theorem appears as `equidistributed_of_cube_differences`
and `equidistributed_of_torus_differences`. The preliminary Fourier criteria
appear as `cube_spectral_difference_from_fourier` and
`cube_weakMixing_difference_from_fourier`. These use all nonzero signed
integer frequency vectors and are stated in arbitrary finite dimension.
In dimension one the positive frequencies in the manuscript suffice because
the negative-frequency character is its conjugate and the norms agree.

Scope of the checked results

The checked part of Theorem 3.1 covers the sufficient hypotheses in (i) and
(iii). The implications (ii) and (iv) use a compact component on a chosen
correlation subsequence. Those converse implications are outside the current
proof library. The finite-dimensional positive-measure construction related
to Lemma 2.9 realizes the needed correlations without forming the exact
Hilbert-space embedding. The certificate proves the topological part of
Lemma 2.10. The discrete-spectrum assertion about its empirical measures
remains outside the proof library. The power, square-root, and phase
statements of Lemma 2.15 are also outside the current scope.

The proof library gives qualitative rotation discrepancy for every irrational
rotation. It does not include the cited algebraic irrational discrepancy
rate in Theorem 2.2 or the introductory classical variants for arbitrary
Hilbert spaces. The paper's closed-cube results and its examples have their
own declarations, with the conventions and geometric changes described above.
